## 1.1.62 प्रत्ययलोपे प्रत्ययलक्षणम्

**Already good:** Clear class-style vṛtti; prakāra of लोप linked to 1.1.60.

- **[high · word-purpose]** Second प्रत्यय (in प्रत्ययलक्षणम्) not explained: only the operation conditioned by the suffix as a suffix survives, not one conditioned by its sounds.
  - Proposed: Why प्रत्ययलक्षणम्? Only what the suffix causes AS a suffix survives, not what its sounds cause: रैकुलम्, गोहितम् — no आय्/अव् (वर्णाश्रयत्वात्).
  - Source (kashika 11062): “प्रत्ययलक्षणमिति किम्? रायः कुलं रैकुलम्। गवे हितं गोहितम्।”
- **[high · word-purpose]** Restating प्रत्यय (already in force) means the WHOLE suffix must be elided; partial elision does not count.
  - Proposed: Why repeat प्रत्यय-? The whole suffix must be gone: in आघ्नीय only the स् of सीयुट् is dropped (7.2.79), so 6.4.37 does not apply by 1.1.62.
  - Source (kashika 11062): “कृत्स्नप्रत्ययलोपे यथा स्यात्। इह मा भूत् — आघ्नीय।”
- **[high · relation]** The next sūtra 1.1.63 न लुमताङ्गस्य restricts this one: after elision by लुक्/श्लु/लुप्, aṅga operations conditioned by the suffix do not take place.
  - Proposed: Read with 1.1.63 न लुमताङ्गस्य: if the suffix was dropped by लुक्/श्लु/लुप्, the aṅga does NOT get the suffix-conditioned operation (गर्गाः, मृष्टः — no guṇa/vṛddhi).
  - Source (kashika 11063): “लुमता शब्देन लुप्ते प्रत्यये यदङ्गम्, तस्य प्रत्ययलक्षणं कार्यं न भवति।”
- **[low · example]** One example shows the rule at work: pada-saṃjñā survives the dropped सुप्/तिङ्.
  - Proposed: Example: अग्निचित्, सोमसुत् — the सुप् is dropped, yet the word is still a pada (1.4.14).
  - Source (kashika 11062): “अग्निचित्, सोमसुत्, अधोगित्यत्र सुप्तिङोर्लुप्तयोः <<सुप्तिङन्तं पदम्>> [[१.४.१४]] इति पदसंज्ञा भवति।”

## 1.4.23 कारके

**Already good:** Correctly identifies the sūtra as an adhikāra.

- **[high · word-purpose]** What कारक means (cause/means of the action) is not taught; the page only says ‘heading’.
  - Proposed: कारक = what brings about the action (क्रियायाः हेतुः). Every saṃjñā 1.4.24–1.4.55 (अपादान …) is given only to such a participant.
  - Source (kashika 14023): “कारकशब्दश्च निमित्तपर्यायः। कारकं हेतुरित्यनर्थान्तरम्। कस्य हेतुः? क्रियायाः।”
- **[high · word-purpose]** Why the heading is needed (counter-example): a non-participant gets no kāraka name.
  - Proposed: Why कारके? वृक्षस्य पर्णं पतति — the tree is only related, not a participant in the falling, so no अपादान; माणवकस्य पितरं पृच्छति — no कर्म.
  - Source (kashika 14023): “कारक इति किम्? वृक्षस्य पर्णं पतति।”
- **[low · relation]** Bhāṣya: कारक is a long (महती) name, so it is meaningful (anvartha).
  - Proposed: A long name like कारक is chosen to be meaningful (अन्वर्थसंज्ञा): only what DOES (साधक) the action is a कारक.
  - Source (bhashya 14023): “कारक इति महती संज्ञा क्रियते”

## 2.3.37 यस्य च भावेन भावलक्षणम्

**Already good:** Vṛtti matches the Kāśikā; सप्तमी anuvṛtti shown.

- **[high · word-purpose]** Why भावेन: the marker must be an action, not a property.
  - Proposed: Why भावेन? The marker must be an ACTION: गोषु दुह्यमानासु गतः. A mere attribute does not count: यो जटाभिः, स भुङ्क्ते — no 7th.
  - Source (kashika 23037): “भावेनेति किम्? यो जटाभिः, स भुङ्क्ते।”
- **[medium · word-purpose]** Why भाव- again in भावलक्षणम्: what is marked must also be an action.
  - Proposed: Why भाव- again in भावलक्षणम्? What is marked must be an action too: यो भुङ्क्ते, स देवदत्तः — the person is identified, not an action, so no 7th.
  - Source (kashika 23037): “पुनर्भावग्रहणं किम्? यो भुङ्क्ते, स देवदत्तः॥”
- **[medium · contradiction]** Card ‘कस्मिन् परे? / when what follows?’ for भावलक्षणम्. It names the other action that is marked, not a following element.
  - Proposed: भावलक्षणम् = the other action marked by this one (क्रियान्तरं लक्ष्यते), not ‘what follows’. Card question: ‘what is marked?’
  - Source (kashika 23037): “यस्य च क्रियया क्रियान्तरं लक्ष्यते, ततो भाववतः सप्तमी विभक्तिर्भवति।”

  - Proposed: Vārttika: also when the worthy act and the unworthy do not (and the reverse): सत्सु तरत्सु असन्त आसते.
  - Source (kaumudi 23037): “सत्सु तरत्सु असन्त आसते ।”
- **[low · example]** One example makes the rule concrete.
  - Proposed: Example: गोषु दुह्यमानासु गतः ‘he left while the cows were being milked’ — the milking marks the time of going.
