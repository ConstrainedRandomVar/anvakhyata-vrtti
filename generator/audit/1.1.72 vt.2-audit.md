## 1. Conditions, from the sources

### C1

Vārttika 1 on 1.1.72: in rules prescribing a samāsa or a pratyaya, tadanta-vidhi is barred (नडादिभ्यः फक् does not reach सूत्रनड). Vārttika 2: except where the qualifier is an ugit or a varṇa (sound). The Bhāṣya's examples: उगितश्च (भवती, अतिभवती) and अत इञ् (दाक्षिः).

> समासप्रत्ययविधौ प्रतिषेधः। समासविधौ प्रत्ययविधौ च प्रतिषेधो वक्तव्यः।  
> — bhashya 11072

> उगिद्वर्णग्रहणवर्जम्। उगिद्ग्रहणं वर्णग्रहणं च वर्जयित्वा। उगिद्ग्रहणमुगितश्च। भवती। अतिभवतौ।  
> — bhashya 11072

> उगिद्वर्णग्रहणवर्जमिति वाच्यम् (म०भा० [[१.१.७२]] वा० २)॥ <<उगितश्च>> [[४.१.६]] इति ङीप्प्रत्ययस्तदन्तादपि भवति। भवती। अतिभवती। वर्णग्रहणम् — <<अत इञ्>> [[४.१.९५]] — दाक्षिः। प्लाक्षिः॥  
> — kashika 11072

### C2

What it requires: (1) a samāsa- or pratyaya-vidhi; (2) the base (prātipadika — or dhātu, see C3) is qualified by a sound or an ugit, so that "what ends in it" is read; (3) the sound is really a sound — not a homonymous dhātu/noun/saṃjñā, not a suffix name (that tadanta is PIS 23), not an उपधा or आदि qualifier, not a बह्वच्/द्व्यच् bahuvrīhi, and not already spelt out with अन्त in the sūtra.

> उगिद्वर्णेति । उगिता वर्णेन च प्रातिपदिकं विशेष्यमित्यर्थः ।  
> — padamanjari 11072

### C3

Scope caveat (dhātu suffix rules): the Śekhara restricts vt.1's pratyaya bar to sūtras that name a prātipadika by its form, and the Kāśikā gives एरच् and ओरावश्यके as plain 1.1.72 examples. On that reading vt.1 never arises in kṛt rules with a sound-qualified dhātu, so vt.2 is not strictly needed there. This audit follows the earlier 1.1.72 audit (vt.1 as the Bhāṣya states it is general to pratyaya-vidhi) and counts those credits licensed, flagged for Harsha.

> सोऽपि निषेधो विशिष्य तत्तद्रूपेण गृहीतप्रातिपदिकसूत्र एव ।  
> — paribhashendushekhar 31

> <<एरच्>> [[३.३.५६]] — इवर्णान्तादच्प्रत्ययो भवति। चयः। जयः। अयः।  
> — kashika 11072
